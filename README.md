## About

*Money, Institutions, and Markets* is a seven-chapter reading companion for the fully online, accelerated version of **Money and Banking**. It covers monetary economics and financial economics as a unified subject — from the nature of money and the history of banking through bond pricing, the yield curve, and derivatives.

The approach is institutions-first: before asking how the Federal Reserve sets interest rates, we ask what problem a central bank is supposed to solve and whether it solves it well. The historical and theoretical foundations are not background material — they are the analytical lens through which the rest of the course makes sense.

## Contents

| Week | Chapter | Topics |
|:----:|---------|--------|
| 1 | The Nature and Price of Money | Functions of money, money supply, regression theorem, quantity theory, cryptocurrencies |
| 2 | How Banking Developed Before Central Banks | Free banking, adverse clearing, Scotland/Canada/Australia, gold standard, Bretton Woods |
| 3 | Central Banks and the Macroeconomy | Origins of central banks, monetary anchor problem, the Fed and ECB, central bank balance sheet |
| 4 | How Central Banks Operate | Rules vs. discretion, monetary policy frameworks, OMOs, corridor/floor systems, bank runs, LOLR |
| 5 | How Financial Markets Work | Interest rates, present value, duration, bond structures, financial system, asymmetric information |
| 6 | Pricing Financial Assets | Bond valuation, yield curve, spot rates, Gordon Growth Model, EMH |
| 7 | Managing Risk in Financial Markets | Risk and return, CAPM, derivatives, financial regulation, exchange rates, Mundell trilemma |
